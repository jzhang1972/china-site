You've read the glossy guidebooks. Now here's the real version.

This isn't a list of every possible thing you could do in Shanghai. It's a 3-day plan that actually works — tested, adjusted, and tested again by someone who's been there. No fluff. Just practical choices with real prices, honest opinions, and tips that save you time and money[citation:9].

## Before You Go: The Three Things That Matter Most

Skip the stress of forgetting something essential. Here's what actually matters before you arrive[citation:9]:

1. **Mobile Payment**: Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before you leave. Link your international card. Shanghai is essentially cashless — you'll use your phone for everything from metro tickets to street food.

2. **Translation App**: Download Baidu Translate or WeChat's built-in translator. You don't need to speak Mandarin, but you do need the right tools[citation:9].

3. **eSIM or VPN**: Google, Instagram, and WhatsApp are blocked. Get a roaming eSIM (which usually bypasses the firewall) or install a VPN before you arrive[citation:9].

## Day 1: The Bund, Old City, and First Impressions

### Morning — The Bund Walk

Start at the Bund (外滩). Walk along the waterfront before the crowds arrive — the colonial-era buildings on one side, Pudong's futuristic skyline on the other.

**Practical tip**: Go early. By 10 AM, it's packed with tour groups.

### Midday — Old City and Yuyuan Garden

Walk south into the Old City. Yuyuan Garden (豫园) is worth the ¥40 entry — it's a genuine Ming Dynasty garden tucked inside the modern city. The surrounding bazaar is touristy but fun for a wander.

**Skip-the-line trick**: Buy tickets online in advance through Trip.com or WeChat mini-programs. The on-site queue can be 30+ minutes on weekends[citation:5].

### Afternoon — Nanjing Road

Walk Nanjing Road from the Bund west. It's a shopping street. You'll either love it or want to escape. Either way, it's the city's spine.

**Food stop**: Xiaolongbao (soup dumplings) at a local spot — not the famous tourist places. Look for a place with a queue of locals.

### Evening — The View

End at the Bund again — but this time for the skyline lit up. It's a different city at night.

**Viewing option**: If you want a high-altitude view, Shanghai Tower charges ¥220 for the top. The Bund's free view is honestly pretty good.

## Day 2: French Concession, Museums, and Local Life

### Morning — French Concession

Skip the tourist buses. Take the metro to South Shaanxi Road and just walk. The French Concession is Shanghai at its most walkable — tree-lined streets, art deco buildings, and cafes tucked into former lane houses.

**Must-see**: Wukang Road. The Wukang Building at the intersection is an iconic 1920s apartment block. Walk down Fuxing Road for the plane trees.

### Midday — Museum or Market

**Option A**: Shanghai Museum (人民广场) — free, but you need to book in advance. The ancient bronze and jade collections are world-class.

**Option B**: Tianzifang (田子坊) — a maze of alleyways with boutiques, galleries, and street food. Tourist-heavy but still fun. Watch your wallet in crowds.

### Afternoon — The High-Speed Rail Demo

Here's something most itineraries miss: take the metro to Hongqiao station just to see it. It's a demonstration of how China moves — a transit hub the size of a small city. You'll see how the high-speed rail system works if you're planning to travel onward[citation:9].

### Evening — Local Dinner

Eat where locals eat. Avoid restaurants with English menus and pictures — they're priced for tourists. Instead:

- **Huanghe Road**: A street of small, no-frills restaurants
- **Local chain**: Look for a place full of office workers in the early evening

## Day 3: Riverside Stroll, Pudong, and Departure

### Morning — The Riverside

Skip the tourist ferries. Walk the Huangpu River path on the Pudong side. The view of the Bund from across the river is better than being on it.

### Midday — Pudong Exploration

Pudong is futuristic and sterile. But the Shanghai Tower (if you didn't go on Day 1) and the adjacent skyscrapers are impressive.

**Money-saving tip**: The Shanghai Tower observation deck isn't the only high view. The hotel bars in the area often have better prices and the same skyline.

### Afternoon — Last-Minute Souvenirs

If you need gifts, skip the tourist shops. Go to a local supermarket — you'll find better snacks, tea, and souvenirs at actual prices.

### Evening — Getting Out

From Shanghai, you can take a high-speed train to most of China. Book tickets in advance through an English platform like Trip.com[citation:9]. Tickets sell out, especially on weekends and holidays.

## How This Itinerary Works

This isn't a "see everything" plan. It's a "see the right things without exhaustion" plan. You'll walk. You'll use the metro (it's excellent). You'll eat well.

The goal isn't to check off attractions. It's to leave Shanghai feeling like you understood the city — not just survived it.
