**Quick Answer:** Use **Didi** (China's Uber) for short trips, the **metro** for city travel, and **high-speed trains** between cities. Download Didi and Amap before you fly. Didi has an English interface, but drivers call you to confirm your location — use the in-app chat to send a translated message. You don't need a physical train ticket: scan your passport at the gate.

---

China has one of the most advanced transportation networks in the world. High-speed trains connect major cities in hours, and ride-hailing apps make getting a taxi simple and affordable.

However, the apps and systems are different from what you use at home. Google Maps doesn't work well, and you can't just hail a cab on the street.

This guide walks you through the setup order — Didi first, then trains, then maps — so you know exactly what to do before and after you land.

---

## Step 1: Set Up Didi Before You Land

Didi is China's equivalent of Uber. It is incredibly convenient, cheap, and has an English interface. You should use Didi instead of trying to hail a taxi on the street.

### How to set up Didi

- [ ] Download the **Didi Rider** app (or use the Didi mini-program inside Alipay).
- [ ] Register using your international phone number.
- [ ] Link your payment method (you can link Alipay or a foreign credit card directly).

### How to book a ride

1. Open the app and enter your destination.
2. The app will show you the price and wait time.
3. Confirm the ride.
4. **Crucial tip:** The driver will likely call you to confirm your location. They usually don't speak English. Use the in-app chat to send a translated message, or just say "Hello" and wait where the pin is.
5. The app will show the car's license plate number. Match it to the car that arrives.

> 📖 **Need to set up Alipay first?** Didi payments run through Alipay. Read our **[China payment guide](/blog/how-to-pay-in-china/)** before you fly.

## Step 2: Book High-Speed Trains the Easy Way

China's high-speed rail network is the largest in the world. It's fast, clean, and often better than flying.

### How to book tickets

- [ ] **The easiest way:** Use **Trip.com** (the international version). It has an English interface, accepts foreign credit cards, and lets you book train tickets easily.
- [ ] **The local way:** Use the official **12306** app, but the English version is limited and it requires a Chinese bank card for payment.

> 📖 **Need a full walkthrough?** Read our **[high-speed train booking guide](/blog/book-high-speed-train-tickets-china-foreigners/)**.

### How to board the train

- [ ] Arrive at the station at least 45 minutes early. Stations are huge.
- [ ] You must go through security (similar to an airport).
- [ ] **No physical ticket needed:** You will scan your passport at the ticket gate to enter. Keep your passport with you at all times.
- [ ] Find your carriage number and seat number on the ticket (or in the app).

---

## Step 3: Install Amap Before You Arrive

Google Maps is mostly useless in China. It has outdated data, doesn't show public transit, and often routes you the wrong way.

### What to download

- [ ] **Amap (高德地图)**: It has an English version, shows accurate bus and metro routes, and includes real-time traffic.
- [ ] **Baidu Maps (百度地图)**: Also very accurate, but primarily in Chinese.

### How to use Amap

- Download the app before you leave or as soon as you land.
- Enter your destination (copy and paste the Chinese address is best).
- The app will show you walking, metro, or driving routes.
- If you are taking a taxi, the driver will usually use their own Amap app.

---

## Step 4: Ride the Metro with Alipay

Major cities like Beijing, Shanghai, and Chengdu have excellent metro systems. They are cheap, fast, and easy to navigate.

- **How to pay:** You don't need to buy a physical ticket. Open your **Alipay** app, tap "Transport" or "Ride Code," and scan the QR code at the metro gate.
- **How to navigate:** Look for the English signs. Most metro stations have English translations for lines and stops.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Trying to hail a taxi on the street.** It rarely works in major cities. Use Didi instead.
- **Expecting the Didi driver to speak English.** They usually don't. Use the in-app chat to send a translated message, and wait where the pin is.
- **Arriving at the train station 10 minutes before departure.** Stations are huge, and you need to go through security. Arrive at least 45 minutes early.
- **Looking for a physical train ticket.** There isn't one. Scan your passport at the gate.
- **Relying on Google Maps.** It has outdated data and no public transit info. Install Amap before you land.
- **Buying a metro ticket from a machine.** You don't need one. Use the Alipay "Transport" QR code at the gate.

## FAQ: Getting Around China

### 1. Is Didi safe for foreigners?
Yes. Didi is the dominant ride-hailing app in China, with driver identity verification and in-app safety features. Match the license plate before you get in.

### 2. Can I use Google Maps in China?
No. Google Maps is blocked and has outdated data. Use Amap or Baidu Maps instead.

### 3. Do I need a Chinese phone number for Didi?
No. You can register with your international phone number. But the driver will call you, so make sure your phone can receive calls or use the in-app chat.

### 4. How do I book high-speed train tickets as a foreigner?
Use Trip.com — it has an English interface and accepts foreign credit cards. The official 12306 app requires a Chinese bank card. Read our full train booking guide for step-by-step instructions.

### 5. Do I need a physical train ticket in China?
No. You scan your passport at the ticket gate. Keep your passport with you at all times.

### 6. How do I pay for the metro in China?
Use Alipay. Open the app, tap "Transport" or "Ride Code," and scan the QR code at the metro gate. No physical ticket needed.
