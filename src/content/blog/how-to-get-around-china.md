China has one of the most advanced transportation networks in the world. High-speed trains connect major cities in hours, and ride-hailing apps make getting a taxi simple and affordable.

However, the apps and systems are different from what you use at home. Google Maps doesn't work well, and you can't just hail a cab on the street.

Here is your complete guide to getting around China like a local.

---

## 1. Ride-Hailing: How to Use Didi (滴滴)

Didi is China's equivalent of Uber. It is incredibly convenient, cheap, and has an English interface. You should use Didi instead of trying to hail a taxi on the street.

**How to set it up:**
- [ ] Download the **Didi Rider** app (or use the Didi mini-program inside Alipay).
- [ ] Register using your international phone number.
- [ ] Link your payment method (you can link Alipay or a foreign credit card directly).

**How to book a ride:**
1. Open the app and enter your destination.
2. The app will show you the price and wait time.
3. Confirm the ride.
4. **Crucial Tip:** The driver will likely call you to confirm your location. They usually don't speak English. Use the in-app chat to send a translated message, or just say "Hello" and wait where the pin is.
5. The app will show the car's license plate number. Match it to the car that arrives.

---

## 2. High-Speed Trains: The Best Way to Travel Between Cities

China's high-speed rail network is the largest in the world. It's fast, clean, and often better than flying.

**How to book tickets:**
- [ ] **The easiest way:** Use **Trip.com** (the international version). It has an English interface, accepts foreign credit cards, and lets you book train tickets easily.
- [ ] **The local way:** Use the official **12306** app, but the English version is limited and it requires a Chinese bank card for payment.

**How to board the train:**
- [ ] Arrive at the station at least 45 minutes early. Stations are huge.
- [ ] You must go through security (similar to an airport).
- [ ] **No physical ticket needed:** You will scan your passport at the ticket gate to enter. Keep your passport with you at all times.
- [ ] Find your carriage number and seat number on the ticket (or in the app).

---

## 3. Navigation: Why You Must Use Amap

Google Maps is mostly useless in China. It has outdated data, doesn't show public transit, and often routes you the wrong way.

**You must download a Chinese map app. The best options are:**
- [ ] **Amap (高德地图)**: It has an English version, shows accurate bus and metro routes, and includes real-time traffic.
- [ ] **Baidu Maps (百度地图)**: Also very accurate, but primarily in Chinese.

**How to use Amap:**
- Download the app before you leave or as soon as you land.
- Enter your destination (copy and paste the Chinese address is best).
- The app will show you walking, metro, or driving routes.
- If you are taking a taxi, the driver will usually use their own Amap app.

---

## 4. Metro and Public Transit

Major cities like Beijing, Shanghai, and Chengdu have excellent metro systems. They are cheap, fast, and easy to navigate.

- **How to pay:** You don't need to buy a physical ticket. Open your **Alipay** app, tap "Transport" or "Ride Code," and scan the QR code at the metro gate.
- **How to navigate:** Look for the English signs. Most metro stations have English translations for lines and stops.

---

## Summary Checklist

Before you fly, make sure you have:

- [ ] Downloaded and set up **Didi**.
- [ ] Downloaded **Amap** or Baidu Maps.
- [ ] Linked your payment method to Didi.
- [ ] Familiarized yourself with how to book trains on Trip.com.
- [ ] Your passport ready for train security checks.
