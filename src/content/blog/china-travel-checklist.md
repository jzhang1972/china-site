**Quick Answer:** Before you fly to China, set up **Alipay** (link your Visa/Mastercard and complete passport verification), buy and install a **China eSIM** that routes data through Hong Kong or Singapore, download **Didi** and **Amap**, and carry **¥200–500 in cash** plus printed hotel addresses in Chinese. Do all of this at home on stable Wi-Fi — app stores and VPN websites are blocked once you're inside China.

---

Traveling to China as a foreigner is an incredible experience, but it requires a little more digital preparation than other countries. China has its own ecosystem of apps, payment methods, and digital services.

If you arrive at the airport without these set up, you'll find yourself stranded, unable to pay for a taxi, or unable to connect to the internet.

This checklist is organized by when you need to do each thing: **before you fly**, **when you land**, and **after you arrive**. Tick each item as you go.

## Before You Fly: Payments

China is virtually cashless. You need to set up at least one mobile payment app before you land.

- [ ] **Download Alipay** and link your international Visa or Mastercard.
- [ ] **Download WeChat** and set up WeChat Pay as a backup.
- [ ] **Complete identity verification** (passport upload and facial scan) inside the apps *before* you travel.
- [ ] **Carry a small amount of cash** (¥200–500 RMB) for emergencies or if your phone dies.

## Before You Fly: Internet & Connectivity

Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, and many other apps are blocked in China. You need a plan for internet access.

- [ ] **Buy a China eSIM** that routes data through Hong Kong or Singapore. This bypasses the firewall — no VPN needed for mobile data. Install it before you fly.
- [ ] **Install a reliable VPN** on your phone and laptop if you plan to use hotel Wi-Fi. Do this before you arrive — VPN websites and app stores are blocked in China.
- [ ] **Download offline maps**. Google Maps doesn't work well in China. Download Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps.
- [ ] **Download a translation app** (like Google Translate with offline Chinese downloaded, or Pleco).

## Before You Fly: Essential Apps

You cannot rely on the apps you use at home. Download these before you fly:

- [ ] **Alipay & WeChat** (Payments & communication)
- [ ] **Didi (滴滴出行)** (China's Uber equivalent. It has an English interface).
- [ ] **Amap (高德地图)** or **Baidu Maps** (Navigation. Amap has an English version but is still mainly Chinese).
- [ ] **Trip.com** (For booking high-speed trains and hotels).

## Before You Fly: Documents & Hardware

- [ ] **Valid Passport & Visa** (Check your visa validity and entry rules).
- [ ] **Power Bank** (You will use your phone constantly for payments and maps. China uses 220V plugs, so bring a universal adapter if needed).
- [ ] **Printed Hotel Addresses in Chinese** (Taxi drivers usually don't speak English, so having the address written in Chinese characters is essential).

## When You Land: First 30 Minutes

- [ ] **Turn on your China eSIM** and enable data roaming for that line. You should see 4G or 5G within a few seconds.
- [ ] **Turn off your home SIM** to avoid roaming charges.
- [ ] **Withdraw ¥800–1,200 in cash** at an airport ATM (ask for smaller bills if possible).
- [ ] **Open Alipay** and test a small payment (a coffee or a metro ticket) to confirm it works.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Starting app verification at the airport.** Alipay verification can take up to 24 hours, and WeChat Pay can take 2–3 days. Do it at home, on stable Wi-Fi.
- **Buying an eSIM after you land.** You may not be able to access the eSIM provider's website inside China. Buy and install it before you fly.
- **Assuming your home apps will work.** Google Maps, WhatsApp, and Instagram are blocked on local networks. Download Amap and a VPN before you leave.
- **Carrying only large bills.** Small shops and taxis may not have change. Ask for smaller bills at the bank counter or airport ATM.
- **Not having your hotel address in Chinese.** Taxi drivers usually don't speak English, and showing them a Chinese address is the fastest way to get where you're going.
- **Relying on hotel Wi-Fi without a VPN.** Hotel Wi-Fi goes through the local network and is blocked by the firewall.

## FAQ: China Travel Checklist

### 1. Do I need a VPN if I buy a China eSIM?
No. A China eSIM that routes data through Hong Kong or Singapore bypasses the firewall. You only need a VPN if you plan to use hotel Wi-Fi or a local Chinese SIM card.

### 2. How much cash should I bring to China?
Withdraw ¥800–1,200 at an airport ATM when you arrive. Most payments are mobile, but cash is a useful backup if your phone dies or a small vendor doesn't accept QR codes.

### 3. Can I set up Alipay after I arrive in China?
You can, but it's not recommended. Verification can take up to 24 hours, and you may need stable internet to complete it. Set it up at home before you fly.

### 4. Do I need a Chinese phone number to use Didi or Alipay?
No. Both apps work with an international phone number. Didi drivers may call you, so make sure your phone can receive calls or use the in-app chat.

### 5. What if I forget to install a VPN before I fly?
VPN websites and app stores are blocked in China. If you forget, you may not be able to install one after you land. Install two different VPNs before you travel as a backup.

### 6. Is Google Maps usable in China?
No. Google Maps is blocked and has outdated data. Download Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps before you fly.
