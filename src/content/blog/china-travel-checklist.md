Traveling to China as a foreigner is an incredible experience, but it requires a little more digital preparation than other countries. China has its own ecosystem of apps, payment methods, and digital services.

If you arrive at the airport without these set up, you'll find yourself stranded, unable to pay for a taxi, or unable to connect to the internet.

Here is your ultimate **China Travel Pre-Departure Checklist** to ensure a smooth, stress-free trip.

---

## 1. Set Up Your Payment Apps (Crucial)

China is virtually cashless. You need to set up at least one mobile payment app before you land.

- [ ] **Download Alipay** and link your international Visa or Mastercard.
- [ ] **Download WeChat** and set up WeChat Pay as a backup.
- [ ] **Complete identity verification** (passport upload and facial scan) inside the apps *before* you travel.
- [ ] **Carry a small amount of cash** (¥200–500 RMB) for emergencies or if your phone dies.

## 2. Prepare Your Internet & Connectivity

Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, and many other apps are blocked in China. You need a plan for internet access.

- [ ] **Buy an eSIM** (like Airalo or Nomad) before you fly, or set up international roaming with your home carrier. *Note: eSIMs usually route data outside of China, bypassing the firewall.*
- [ ] **Install a reliable VPN** on your phone and laptop if you plan to use hotel Wi-Fi. (Do this before you arrive, as VPN websites are blocked in China).
- [ ] **Download offline maps**. Google Maps doesn't work well in China. Download a map app that works locally.
- [ ] **Download a translation app** (like Google Translate with offline Chinese downloaded, or Pleco).

## 3. Essential Apps to Download

You cannot rely on the apps you use at home. Download these before you fly:

- [ ] **Alipay & WeChat** (Payments & communication)
- [ ] **Didi (滴滴出行)** (China's Uber equivalent. It has an English interface).
- [ ] **Amap (高德地图)** or **Baidu Maps** (Navigation. Amap has an English version but is still mainly Chinese).
- [ ] **Trip.com** (For booking high-speed trains and hotels).

---

## 4. Important Documents & Hardware

- [ ] **Valid Passport & Visa** (Check your visa validity and entry rules).
- [ ] **Power Bank** (You will use your phone constantly for payments and maps. China uses 220V plugs, so bring a universal adapter if needed).
- [ ] **Printed Hotel Addresses in Chinese** (Taxi drivers usually don't speak English, so having the address written in Chinese characters is essential).

## Final Thoughts

Preparation is everything. If you tick off this checklist before you board your plane, you'll be able to navigate China like a pro.

Once your payments and internet are set up, you can relax and enjoy the incredible food, history, and culture.
