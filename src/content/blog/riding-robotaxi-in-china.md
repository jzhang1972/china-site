*This is a story from one of our recent trips. It’s not a standard travel guide, but a look at what the future of transportation feels like today.*

---

I stood on the side of a busy street in Wuhan, holding my phone, waiting for a car that didn't have a driver.

My Chinese friend had used his app to book a Robotaxi for me. “Don’t worry,” he said. “The car will stop, you get in, and it drives itself. Just remember to buckle up.”

As an international traveler, I had heard a lot about China’s high-speed trains and Alipay. But I had no idea that driverless taxis were already a normal part of daily life here.

This is what it was like.

## Why I Couldn't Book It Myself

Before my trip, I had tried to download the local Robotaxi app. 

It didn’t work. Most of these apps require a Chinese ID card or a +86 phone number for verification. Some travelers report that they can use a passport, but the process is slow and can take days to approve. If you don't speak fluent Chinese, the interface is also a barrier.

So, my advice for any foreign traveler who wants to try a Robotaxi: **Ask a local friend, a hotel concierge, or a tour guide to book it for you.** 

It’s frustrating that the technology is here but the app is not entirely tourist-friendly yet. But it’s definitely worth the effort to experience it once.

---

## The Ride Experience

When the car arrived, it was a white SUV. The roof had a huge sensor array that looked like a small spaceship.

There was no one in the driver's seat. The steering wheel moved on its own.

I opened the door using the app (my friend’s phone, technically), and got in.

**The safety check:** Before the car started moving, I had to click a button to confirm that I had my seatbelt on. The car won't start until you do.

**The driving:** It was smoother than I expected. The car drove exactly at the speed limit, changed lanes cautiously, and stopped very gently at red lights. It was much more polite than most human drivers in China.

**The silence:** The biggest difference was the silence. There was no radio, no driver making small talk. Just the quiet hum of the electric motor and the occasional beep from the sensor system.

For a 5-kilometer trip, the cost was only **12 yuan** (about $1.70). That’s incredibly cheap. If I took a normal taxi, it would have been at least 25 yuan.

> 💡 **Planning to use ride-hailing in China?** Robotaxis are still hard for foreigners to book, but you can easily use Didi (China's Uber) with your international phone number. Read our guide on **[How to Get Around China](/blog/how-to-get-around-china/)** to learn how to set it up.

## Is It Safe?

I felt completely safe. The car had sensors on all sides, and a remote human operator monitors the fleet, ready to take over if anything goes wrong. 

But I will say this: it takes a few minutes to get used to the fact that the steering wheel is turning and there are no hands on it. Once you relax and look out the window, it feels like the future has already arrived.

---

## Should You Add This to Your Itinerary?

If you are visiting **Wuhan, Beijing (Yizhuang area), or Shenzhen**, and you want to see something that will genuinely blow your mind, try a Robotaxi.

Just don't try to be a hero and use your own foreign passport. **Ask for help from a local, and take the ride.**

## The Bigger Picture: Technology and Travel

This experience made me think about how fast China is moving. While tourists still struggle to set up Alipay and eSIMs, the country has already moved on to driverless cars.

It’s a reminder that for foreign travelers, **preparation is everything**. You need the right payment apps, the right internet setup, and sometimes, you just need to ask a local for help.
