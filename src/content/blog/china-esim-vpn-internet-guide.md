Traveling to China is an incredible experience, but the internet works very differently here. Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube are all blocked by the Great Firewall.

If you arrive at the airport expecting to open Google Maps or message your family on WhatsApp, you'll be stuck.

Here is your complete guide to staying connected in China, including the best eSIM options and how to set up a VPN before you fly.

---

## 1. The Best Option: Travel eSIM (No VPN Needed)

For most international travelers, a travel eSIM is the easiest and most reliable way to get online in China.

**Why it works:** Travel eSIMs route your data through servers outside of China (usually in Hong Kong or Singapore). Because the data originates outside the firewall, **you do not need a VPN to access Google, WhatsApp, or Instagram.** You get full, unrestricted access to the global internet.

**How to install and activate your eSIM:**

1. **Before you fly (on home Wi-Fi):**
   - Buy the eSIM and check your email for the QR code.
   - Go to your phone settings → Cellular / Mobile Data → Add eSIM.
   - Scan the QR code. (Do not delete the eSIM from your phone).
   - Label it "China Travel eSIM".

2. **When you land in China:**
   - Turn off your home SIM card (to avoid expensive roaming charges).
   - Turn on the "China Travel eSIM".
   - Enable **"Data Roaming"** for this eSIM line (this is crucial — it will not work without it).
   - Wait a few seconds for it to connect to the local network. You should see 4G or 5G.

3. **Troubleshooting:**
   - If it doesn't work, restart your phone.
   - Check if the Access Point Name (APN) needs to be set manually (the eSIM provider will give you the APN details in the email).
   - Make sure you are not connected to a hotel Wi-Fi network that requires a Chinese phone number to log in.

⚠️ **Note:** Travel eSIMs usually only provide data. You will not get a Chinese phone number, but you can use apps like WeChat or WhatsApp for calls and messages.

## 2. The Alternative: Local SIM Card

If you need a Chinese phone number (for example, to register for certain local services or food delivery apps), you can buy a physical SIM card at the airport upon arrival.

**How to get one:**
- Look for China Mobile, China Unicom, or China Telecom counters in the arrival hall.
- You must show your passport to buy a SIM card.
- The staff will help you install it and register it.

⚠️ **Crucial Warning:** Local Chinese SIM cards use the local network, which means **you will be blocked by the firewall**. You will need a VPN to access Google, Instagram, and WhatsApp.

---

## 3. The Essential Tool: A Reliable VPN

If you plan to use hotel Wi-Fi or a local SIM card, installing a VPN is mandatory.

**Why you must install it before you fly:**
VPN websites and app stores are blocked in China. You cannot download a VPN once you are inside the country. You must download and install it on your phone and laptop *before* you leave.

**Tips for choosing a VPN:**
- Look for VPNs known to work reliably in China (e.g., ExpressVPN, NordVPN, Surfshark, LetsVPN).
- Download two different VPNs as a backup, in case one is having connection issues.
- Test the VPN on your devices before you travel.

## 4. Apps to Download Before You Go

Even with an eSIM, you need the right apps to navigate China.

- [ ] **Alipay & WeChat** (Payments and daily communication).
- [ ] **A Translation App**: Download Google Translate and download the offline Chinese language pack. Also consider downloading Pleco, a highly-rated Chinese dictionary app.
- [ ] **Maps**: Google Maps does not work well in China and lacks public transit data. You will need to download Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps (百度地图) once you arrive.
