I didn't know what to expect when I was told I'd be attending a "royal banquet" in Chongqing. The city is famous for its hotpot, its neon skyline, and its monorail that punches through buildings. A quiet, ancient feast was the last thing on my mind.

Then I walked into the **Huali Banquet (华礼宴·礼宴巴国)** in the Ba Kingdom Park, and the noise of the modern city disappeared.

The entrance was a grand, red gate. I was greeted by attendants in flowing Hanfu robes, who bowed and led me inside. I was offered a costume to change into, but I chose to simply observe as a visitor, not a participant. The main hall was a revelation. The tables were arranged in a "T" shape around a central stage, just like in an ancient palace. The screens around the room projected scenes of ancient Bashu, and the air smelled of incense and the promise of a story.

The experience, known as the "Banquet of the Ba Kingdom" (*礼宴巴国*), is a 90-minute journey through the dynasties. It's a performance where you are not just a diner, but a guest at a royal court. It's a dining concept that has become a cultural phenomenon, with some guests booking their seats months in advance.

**And it all starts with a different kind of map.**

## Why Is Everyone Suddenly Talking About Chongqing?

If you have scrolled through TikTok or Instagram recently, you have probably seen Chongqing. The city went viral for its "8D" skyline, monorails that fly through buildings, and a certain impersonator named **Ryan Chen** who calls himself the **"Chinese Trump."**

Ryan Chen is a Chongqing local whose videos mocking the former US president have racked up millions of views. In one of his most famous clips, he invites the real Donald Trump to visit Chongqing and try the local hotpot. The videos have been covered by CNN, the Times of India, and dozens of other international outlets.

Because of creators like Ryan Chen, millions of foreign travelers now know about Chongqing. They come for the cyberpunk photos at Liziba Station and the neon lights of Hongya Cave.

But after you have taken the photo and posted it, what's next?

For travelers who want to go beyond the viral clips, there is a quieter, older Chongqing waiting to be discovered. And the best place to find it is at a banquet table.

## A Map on a Plate

This is not a map of roads. It's a map of **China's gastronomic history**, told through a single meal.

Each dish is a chapter in the story of Chinese cuisine. The menu is presented as a series of courses, with narrators and actors in ancient dress telling the story behind each one. I realized I was being guided through the geography of Chinese flavors, but not just by region. By *time*.

I traveled from the spicy, robust flavors of the Ba Kingdom, the ancient state that once ruled this land, to the refined elegance of the Han, Tang, Song, and Ming dynasties. Each dish was a snapshot of a culinary era.

The tasting menu was a tribute to a lost world of cookery, a rare opportunity to taste dishes that have been passed down through dynasties. This is not just a meal; it's a journey through the culinary history of the Ba Kingdom, a culture that once ruled the land that is now modern Chongqing and Sichuan province.

It started with a clear soup with a chicken meatball, which the host explained was a dish as old as the dynasty itself. Then came a piece of glazed pork belly, its skin lacquered until it shattered with a spoon. It was the ancestor of the pork dishes that would one day travel the world. A Sichuan peppercorn-crusted fish arrived, a reminder that the numbing spice has been a signature of the region for centuries. Each course was presented with an explanation, linking the ingredients to the history of the land.

One dish, named after the ancient Ba Kingdom, was a modern interpretation of a "鼎罐" (ding-guan) stew. This was a connection to the days when locals would gather around a single pot to share a meal, a practice that is often cited as the spiritual ancestor of the modern hotpot culture. When the last course was served, I realized I hadn't just eaten a meal. I had traced the evolution of a cuisine through time.

## The Performance of the People

The meal was synced to a stunning stage show. Actors in ancient armor, noble robes, and flowing silk danced and performed scenes of poetry, warfare, and court life. The performance wasn't just a background to the meal. It was a visual guide to the food on my plate.

One minute, I was watching a dance of the Tang Dynasty, elegant and slow. The next, I was hearing the powerful chants of the *Chuanjiang Haozi*, the work songs of the Yangtze River boatmen, a reminder that the food of Chongqing was forged by the labor and spirit of its people.

It was a reminder that this "royal banquet" was, in its own way, a celebration of the common people. That the maps of history, culture, and flavor are all drawn by the same hands.

This experience is not just a meal; it's a journey through the complex history and flavors that make up China's diverse food culture.

Like the "Map of Chinese Flavors" article, this banquet celebrates the diversity of Chinese cuisine. ## Where Is the Banquet Hall in Chongqing?

The banquet hall in Chongqing that hosts the Ba Kingdom feast is located at the **Huali Banquet (华礼宴·礼宴巴国)**, inside **Ba Kingdom Park (巴国城)** in the Jiulongpo District.

**Address (Chinese):** 重庆市九龙坡区红狮大道巴国城公园内

**How to get there:** Take a taxi or Didi (China's ride-hailing app) and show the driver the Chinese address above. The park is about 30 minutes from Chongqing's city center.

**When to arrive:** Doors open about 30 minutes before the performance. Arrive early if you want to take photos in the traditional courtyard before the banquet begins.

**Parking:** Available at the Ba Kingdom Park.

## What Is the Royal Banquet of Chongqing?

The "Royal Banquet of Chongqing" and the "Banquet of the Ba Kingdom" refer to the same experience. It is also known by its Chinese name, **礼宴巴国 (Li Yan Ba Guo)**.

**Why the different names?**
- **Banquet of the Ba Kingdom:** the historical name, referring to the ancient Ba state that once ruled this region.
- **Royal Banquet:** the marketing name used by tour platforms, emphasizing the imperial-style performance.
- **Huali Banquet (华礼宴):** the venue's brand name.

If you see any of these names when booking, they all point to the same 90-minute dinner show in Chongqing.

**How much does the Banquet of the Ba Kingdom cost?**
The banquet experience is priced at around ¥200-400 per person, depending on the package. Reservations are recommended.

**How do I book the Ba Kingdom Banquet in Chongqing?**
You can book your tickets online in advance. Many visitors reserve their spots weeks or even months ahead.

**Is the Banquet of the Ba Kingdom suitable for foreigners?**
Yes. The experience is designed for all guests, with live performances and storytelling that transcend language. Even if you don't speak Chinese, the visual and culinary experience is unforgettable.

**What is included in the Ba Kingdom Banquet?**
The package includes a multi-course tasting menu, a stage performance with traditional music and dance, and a guided journey through the history of the Ba Kingdom.

**Is there a dress code?**
There is no strict dress code, but many guests choose to wear traditional Chinese clothing (Hanfu). The venue offers costume rental if you want to participate.

**Is this the same Chongqing that went viral with the "Chinese Trump" videos?**
Yes. Ryan Chen, the Chongqing local known as the "Chinese Trump," helped put the city on the global map. The banquet is a way to experience the older, deeper side of Chongqing that the viral clips rarely show.

**Is Huali Banquet the same as the Chongqing Palace Banquet?**
Yes. "Huali Banquet," "Chongqing Palace Banquet," and "Banquet of the Ba Kingdom" are different names for the same venue and experience. If you see any of these names on a booking platform, they all point to the same dinner show in Ba Kingdom Park.

**Is the banquet suitable for families with children?**
Yes, families are welcome. The performance is visual and engaging for children, and the venue can accommodate dietary preferences. However, the experience lasts around 90 minutes, so very young children may find it long.
