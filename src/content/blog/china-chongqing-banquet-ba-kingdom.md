Chongqing is famous for its hotpot, its neon skyline, and its monorail that punches through buildings. A quiet, ancient feast is not the first thing most visitors associate with the city.

Then you walk into the **Huali Banquet (华礼宴·礼宴巴国)** in Ba Kingdom Park, and the noise of the modern city disappears.

The entrance is a grand red gate. Attendants in flowing Hanfu robes bow and lead guests inside. Visitors can rent a costume and change into it, or simply observe as a guest. The main hall is arranged with tables in a "T" shape around a central stage, the way an ancient palace would be set. Screens around the room project scenes of ancient Bashu, and the air smells of incense.

The experience, known as the "Banquet of the Ba Kingdom" (*礼宴巴国*), is a 90-minute journey through the dynasties. It is a performance where guests are not just diners, but guests at a royal court. Some visitors book their seats months in advance.

**And it all starts with a different kind of map.**

## Why Is Everyone Suddenly Talking About Chongqing?

If you have scrolled through TikTok or Instagram recently, you have probably seen Chongqing. The city went viral for its "8D" skyline, monorails that fly through buildings, and a certain impersonator named **Ryan Chen** who calls himself the **"Chinese Trump."**

Ryan Chen is a Chongqing local whose videos mocking the former US president have racked up millions of views. In one of his most famous clips, he invites the real Donald Trump to visit Chongqing and try the local hotpot. The videos have been covered by CNN, the Times of India, and dozens of other international outlets.

Because of creators like Ryan Chen, millions of foreign travelers now know about Chongqing. They come for the cyberpunk photos at Liziba Station and the neon lights of Hongya Cave.

But after the photo is taken and posted, what's next?

For travelers who want to go beyond the viral clips, there is a quieter, older Chongqing waiting to be discovered. And the best place to find it is at a banquet table.

## A Map on a Plate

This is not a map of roads. It is a map of **China's gastronomic history**, told through a single meal.

Each dish is a chapter in the story of Chinese cuisine. The menu is presented as a series of courses, with narrators and actors in ancient dress telling the story behind each one. The meal guides guests through the geography of Chinese flavors, and not just by region, but by time.

The journey runs from the spicy, robust flavors of the Ba Kingdom, the ancient state that once ruled this land, to the refined elegance of the Han, Tang, Song, and Ming dynasties. Each dish is a snapshot of a culinary era.

The tasting menu is a tribute to a lost world of cookery, a rare opportunity to taste dishes passed down through dynasties. This is not just a meal. It is a journey through the culinary history of the Ba Kingdom, a culture that once ruled the land that is now modern Chongqing and Sichuan province.

The menu opens with a clear soup with a chicken meatball, which the host explains is a dish as old as the dynasty itself. Then comes a piece of glazed pork belly, its skin lacquered until it shatters with a spoon, the ancestor of the pork dishes that would one day travel the world. A Sichuan peppercorn-crusted fish arrives, a reminder that the numbing spice has been a signature of the region for centuries. Each course is presented with an explanation linking the ingredients to the history of the land.

One dish, named after the ancient Ba Kingdom, is a modern interpretation of a "鼎罐" (ding-guan) stew. This connects to the days when locals would gather around a single pot to share a meal, a practice often cited as the spiritual ancestor of modern hotpot culture. By the last course, the meal has traced the evolution of a cuisine through time.

## The Performance of the People

The meal is synced to a stage show. Actors in ancient armor, noble robes, and flowing silk perform scenes of poetry, warfare, and court life. The performance is not just a background to the meal. It is a visual guide to the food on the plate.

One moment, a dance of the Tang Dynasty, elegant and slow. The next, the powerful chants of the *Chuanjiang Haozi*, the work songs of the Yangtze River boatmen, a reminder that the food of Chongqing was forged by the labor and spirit of its people.

It is a reminder that this "royal banquet" is, in its own way, a celebration of the common people. That the maps of history, culture, and flavor are all drawn by the same hands.

This experience is not just a meal. It is a journey through the complex history and flavors that make up China's diverse food culture.

Like the "Map of Chinese Flavors" article, this banquet celebrates the diversity of Chinese cuisine. It is a journey to the very heart of this map, where food is not just sustenance, but a living story.

## Where Is the Banquet Hall in Chongqing?

The banquet hall in Chongqing that hosts the Ba Kingdom feast is located at the **Huali Banquet (华礼宴·礼宴巴国)**, inside **Ba Kingdom Park (巴国城)** in the Jiulongpo District.

**Address (Chinese):** 重庆市九龙坡区红狮大道巴国城公园内

**How to get there:** Take a taxi or Didi (China's ride-hailing app) and show the driver the Chinese address above. The park is about 30 minutes from Chongqing's city center.

**When to arrive:** Doors open about 30 minutes before the performance. Arrive early if you want to take photos in the traditional courtyard before the banquet begins.

**Parking:** Available at the Ba Kingdom Park.

## What Is the Royal Banquet of Chongqing?

The "Royal Banquet of Chongqing" and the "Banquet of the Ba Kingdom" refer to the same experience. It is also known by its Chinese name, **礼宴巴国 (Li Yan Ba Guo)**.

**Why the different names?**
- **Banquet of the Ba Kingdom:** the historical name, referring to the ancient Ba state that once ruled this region.
- **Royal Banquet:** the marketing name used by tour platforms, emphasizing the imperial-style performance.
- **Huali Banquet (华礼宴):** the venue's brand name.

If you see any of these names when booking, they all point to the same 90-minute dinner show in Chongqing.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Showing up without a reservation.** This is a seated dinner show with limited capacity. Some visitors book weeks or months in advance, especially on weekends.
- **Arriving right at showtime.** Doors open about 30 minutes early. Arriving early gives you time to photograph the courtyard and find your table without rushing.
- **Not confirming the venue name on your booking.** "Huali Banquet," "Chongqing Palace Banquet," and "Banquet of the Ba Kingdom" are the same place. If a booking platform uses a different name, confirm the address before paying.
- **Expecting an English-language performance.** The narration and songs are in Chinese. The visual storytelling carries the experience, but do not expect a translated script.
- **Assuming the address is easy to find.** The venue is inside Ba Kingdom Park, about 30 minutes from the city center. Show the driver the Chinese address, not the English name.

**How much does the Banquet of the Ba Kingdom cost?**
The banquet experience is priced at around ¥200-400 per person, depending on the package. Reservations are recommended.

**How do I book the Ba Kingdom Banquet in Chongqing?**
You can book your tickets online in advance. Many visitors reserve their spots weeks or even months ahead.

**Is the Banquet of the Ba Kingdom suitable for foreigners?**
Yes. The experience is designed for all guests, with live performances and storytelling that do not depend on language. Even without Chinese, the visual and culinary experience is memorable.

**What is included in the Ba Kingdom Banquet?**
The package includes a multi-course tasting menu, a stage performance with traditional music and dance, and a guided journey through the history of the Ba Kingdom.

**Is there a dress code?**
There is no strict dress code, but many guests choose to wear traditional Chinese clothing (Hanfu). The venue offers costume rental if you want to participate.

**Is this the same Chongqing that went viral with the "Chinese Trump" videos?**
Yes. Ryan Chen, the Chongqing local known as the "Chinese Trump," helped put the city on the global map. The banquet is a way to experience the older, deeper side of Chongqing that the viral clips rarely show.

**Is Huali Banquet the same as the Chongqing Palace Banquet?**
Yes. "Huali Banquet," "Chongqing Palace Banquet," and "Banquet of the Ba Kingdom" are different names for the same venue and experience. If you see any of these names on a booking platform, they all point to the same dinner show in Ba Kingdom Park.

**Is the banquet suitable for families with children?**
Yes, families are welcome. The performance is visual and engaging for children, and the venue can accommodate dietary preferences. The experience lasts around 90 minutes, so very young children may find it long.
